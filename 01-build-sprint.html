Build sprint · 09:45–11:15 · 90 min

Ship a validation artefact

By 11:15, every project has a live link.

The prototype is evidence, not product. A validation artefact makes a customer do something you can count. It’s built in hours, polished just enough to be credible, and success is a clear answer, yes or no. If nobody can act on it, it’s a render, not a test.

1 · Pick one artefact

Landing page with a call to action

For services, software, platforms. Tests whether the promise makes people act.

CTA: join the waiting list, book a call, pre-order.

Clickable prototype

For tools and apps where the workflow is the value. Tests whether people get it and want it.

Use it inside your interviews.

Concept page with renders

For physical, spatial and built-environment projects. Tests demand before you fabricate anything.

CTA: pre-order, pilot request, letter of intent.

2 · Six parts, one action

  1. Headline: your value proposition, in the customer’s words
  2. Sub-headline: for whom, and the main benefit
  3. Three benefits: outcomes, not features
  4. A visual: render, mock-up or sketch, labelled as a concept
  5. One call to action. Only one.
  6. Measurement: visits and sign-ups you can count
WeakAn email
StrongerA booked call
StrongestA pre-order or signed LOI

The CTA should ask for something that costs the visitor a little.
